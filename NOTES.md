# NOTES.md

## Design notes

These notes document the layout decisions behind `entry.png`, THE DAY, ALL OF IT:
a cream one-page broadsheet edition for Thursday 19 November 2026. The page
carries 40 stories drawn from 7 regions, with a stat band, a visible region
tally, and a date-check footer that keeps the edition anchored to its day.
Every design choice below supports that complete daily record, from the red
date tab and numbered stories to the footer separating Thursday's events from
material that merely carried over from the day before.

1. **Cream paper, not white.** The background is a warm newsprint cream
   because the format is quoting the physical broadsheet: white reads as a
   default web canvas, while cream signals "printed edition" at a glance and
   keeps the long list of 38 items easy on the eyes.

2. **The red date tab in the corner.** The top-left red block holding "19
   NOV 2026" is the single most important fact on the page - which day this
   is - so it gets the only solid red field, where a paper would put its
   editions marker. It anchors the eye before the masthead and doubles as a
   corner clip a reader could thumb.

3. **Eight-cell numbers strip.** "The day in numbers" sits directly under
   the masthead because scale belongs before narrative: 9–6, $81bn, $2tn,
   M5.7, 3.6%, EUR45bn, 6–51, 56–44 give one figure per theme (vote, IPO,
   market cap, quake, inflation, loan, two sports) so a skimmer gets the
   day's magnitude in five seconds. Eight cells fill the full width evenly
   and force discipline - a stat had to earn its cell.

4. **Region color bar.** The strip of purple/blue/brown/red/green tabs
   (Asia 8, Europe 8, Africa 7, Americas 7, US 3, Oceania 3, Middle East 2)
   shows the page's geographic spread before any headline is read, and the
   same colors reappear on every card tab and brief number so each story
   stays traceable to its region.

5. **Ranking into hero / secondary / tertiary / briefs.** Stories were
   ranked by global consequence and "happened today" novelty, then spread
   across regions so no two heroes share one. The three heroes (Shenzhen
   peace declaration, ICJ Myanmar genocide ruling, Anthropic IPO) carry
   full headlines plus decks because they are the day's spine. The five
   secondary cards keep headlines only; the seven tertiary cards run short
   headline fragments; the 24 evening briefs compress to one region-tagged
   line each. Depth of treatment tracks importance, so the page reads
   top-to-bottom in decreasing weight.

6. **Numbered stories 1–38.** Every story carries an index number, which
   makes the "38 stories / 7 regions" count in the masthead verifiable at
   a glance and lets briefs reference the same numbering.

7. **Attribution lines.** Heroes and cards end with "REPORTED REUTERS"-
   style source lines, and the briefs use tag codes (STANDARD, BBC, Kyodo,
   ...), so nothing on the page is unattributed - a newspaper convention
   that also answers "says who?" in the smallest possible space.

8. **The date-check footer.** The contest asked for one specific day, so
   the footer is the page's honesty device: it states the kill-as-not-
   Thursday rule (Myanmar fighting, Gaza push, and the 144-drone Kyiv
   attack all happened Wednesday and are excluded), lists sources, and
   notes that disputed claims are attributed rather than asserted. It
   separates the day's real events from carryover, closing the page the
   way the red tab opened it: with the date.
